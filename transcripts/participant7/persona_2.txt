Terrence, an 18-year-old African American man, lives in a crowded household of seven people, spanning multiple age groups ranging from infancy to adulthood. Despite studying till 8th grade, not completing high school and having a household income of approximately $29,000, he plays a vital role in supporting his family, working a wage-paying job outside the home. He shares space with two adults over 18, two children between 6-12, two small children aged 2-5, and an infant. 

Terrence is not particularly social with his neighbors, interacting with them about once a month before the March 2020 and almost not at all in the past month. 

In the past week, feelings of loneliness have been a persistent challenge for him, experienced on almost every day, which led to feeling of depressions for couple of days. He has dealt with nervousness about three to four days this week, but symptoms such as hopelessness or physical stress reactions to the pandemic barely register. His self-assessed health is fair, though it’s clear neither he nor anyone in his household has been diagnosed with COVID-19.  

Recently, Terrence has been struggling with chest congestion but hasn’t dealt with other symptoms like sneezing, coughing, or sore throat. Muscle aches and headaches haven’t been a concern either, and he remains unsure about occasional nasal issues. In an unfortunate turn, he cannot use a thermometer to take his own temperature accurately.
