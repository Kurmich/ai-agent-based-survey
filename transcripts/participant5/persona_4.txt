Margaret is a 35-year-old white woman with a household income of $96,000. She completed her education up to the 9th grade and lives in a bustling household of six, including herself. Two adults, a three year old toddler, two grade-schoolers aged between 6-12, and a sixteen years old teenager share her living space. 

Margaret connected with her neighbors a few times a month, both before and since the pandemic began. While she remained physically unaffected by virus-related anxiety—experiencing it less than one day in the last week—she struggled emotionally. Depression and loneliness weighted heavily on her almost every day, leaving her hopeless for about half of the week. Nonetheless, Margaret stays active through self-employment in her small business. Although her general health is fair, Margaret hasn’t been formally diagnosed with COVID-19, neither has anyone in her household.

In recent days, Margaret has dealt with a few mild health concerns, including a runny or stuffy nose, a sore throat, and occasional coughing. Her thermometer reading reflects a slightly low body temperature at 96.1 degrees Fahrenheit. Despite these symptoms, she hasn’t felt muscle aches, headaches, chills, or sneezing, allowing her to focus on the many responsibilities of her crowded household and business endeavors.
